# Daily guidance backend

`GET /v1/recommendations?section=1&group=4` returns a server-ranked view of the current day for the web client and a future Android client. It reads the unified calendar, reviewed syllabi, current menu, the cached dining recommendation and cached weather. It makes no network or AI request during a read. The response includes a headline, ranked suggestions, separate large and smaller task lists, source freshness, a reason and source evidence for each suggestion. Refresh at most once a minute while the app is visible. `POST /v1/recommendations/actions` accepts a suggestion `id` with `done`, `undo` or `snooze` (`until` in epoch milliseconds); the backend saves this state across devices.

Ranking changes with the clock. A class in progress and an imminent quiz outrank lunch. Food moves up near lunch only when today's menu exists. Larger assignments appear several days before their deadline. Schedule gaps suggest a possible study window without claiming it is enough time to finish the work. A scheduled office hour becomes relevant when the same course has an approaching task. Overlapping events get a conflict warning. Weather appears from the cached forecast when rain, cold, heat or wind may affect the next trip. Old or failed source data stays labeled.

The engine separates facts from suggestions. LEARN supplies exact deadlines and links when available. A reviewed syllabus can supply topic coverage, readings and assessment dates. A quiz's coverage is shown only when an explicitly matching syllabus assessment or the LEARN description names that content. Generic calendar events, including events for unspecified programs or streams, remain visible in the calendar without becoming confirmed tasks or commitments. Week ranges are labeled as period content; they are not presented as a certain lecture topic. A syllabus date without a due time stays date-only. The service does not assume an overdue item is incomplete, that an outlet is open, or that a study window guarantees completion.

## Syllabus import

`POST /v1/courses/<course>/syllabus/preview` accepts `{ "text": "...", "term_start": "2026-09-08", "year": 2026, "use_ai": false }`. Text, Markdown, copied tables and HTML are supported. `term_start` anchors numbered weeks to the first day of Week 1. The response is a reviewed document with dated topic, reading and assessment entries, source evidence and warnings for missing dates. Explicit chapter, section, unit, module, lecture and week ranges on an assessment are kept as coverage. `use_ai` must be a boolean; `true` can find additional source-backed rows when a Cloudflare AI binding is available. The parser verifies AI evidence against the pasted text and falls back to the rules parser when AI is unavailable. `PUT /v1/courses/<course>/syllabus` saves the reviewed document; `GET` reads it. A scanned PDF requires extracted text or OCR before import.

The calendar API includes syllabus topic/readings by day and syllabus assessments alongside the live feeds. LEARN's matching assessment wins to keep its live time and submission link. Imported plans can be edited or replaced; a review step prevents tentative syllabus text from becoming a deadline without inspection.

## Free allocation and load

The recommendation endpoint uses stored facts and costs no AI neurons. The Worker runs one cron trigger per minute; source jobs retain their own cadences of one minute for status, six hours for the Google schedule calendar, 15 minutes for LEARN, six hours for office hours, and 12 hours for menus. Weather refreshes every 30 minutes into D1. Changed menu and alert text can trigger cached AI work; syllabus AI runs only when explicitly requested. A shared D1 reservation caps this app's conservative estimated AI use at **7,000 neurons per UTC day**, leaving headroom below Cloudflare's [10,000-neuron free daily allowance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/). Each actual AI attempt, including a validation retry, reserves its maximum output and a conservative input estimate before calling the model. The allowance resets after midnight UTC. Food also has a 12-attempt daily cap and alerts an eight-attempt cap.

Manual dining ranking, office-hours parsing, and AI syllabus review are server-owned jobs. Their POST routes save a processing record and return immediately; the Worker continues through `waitUntil`, while the local relay runs the same job independently of the browser connection. Clients read the saved status and result after a refresh. A stalled Worker job is retried by cron, and the local relay checks stalled jobs even when source polling is disabled. The saved dining profile is the sole ranking input, and a manual ranking holds its menu signature so automatic ranking does not duplicate the AI call.

`GET /v1/ai/usage` reports the app's reserved allowance and reset time. It is an upper estimate, not Cloudflare's account-wide meter. Other Workers on the same account can also use that account's allocation; the Cloudflare dashboard remains the source of truth for account totals.

The cron processes at most two due sources per invocation. A test exercises a large source poll followed by fresh alert, dining and weather work under the [50 D1 queries per Worker invocation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/). The stored run log is indexed; unreferenced snapshots older than seven days are pruned. Google Calendar 429 responses back off for at least 12 hours, double on repeated failures, and honor a longer upstream `Retry-After` value.

## Day simulation

Run `node tools/simulate-recommendations.mjs` for a fixed synthetic Wednesday. At 08:00 the next class leads, at 09:10 the in-progress class leads, at 11:45 lunch leads, at 14:05 the tutorial leads, and at 20:00 an approaching quiz leads. A weekend scenario promotes a large project and a free study window. Automated scenarios also check that an imminent quiz beats lunch, completed or snoozed tasks disappear, a changed due date resurfaces, missing quiz coverage is not invented, yesterday's menu is excluded, stale data is labeled and schedule conflicts are reported.
